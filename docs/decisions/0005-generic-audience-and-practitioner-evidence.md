# 0005: Mainworthy is for teams in general; the owner's career is evidence, not the customer

- Date: 2026-10-10
- Status: accepted (the owner)

Answers the open part of question 1 and corrects an assumption in the first
draft of `docs/ux/flows.md`, which treated the owner's current team as the
reference user.

## Decision

1. **The audience is software QA teams in general**, from a solo tester or a
   startup to a large enterprise (scope: decision 0003). Mainworthy is a
   product others adopt. No team, including the owner's, is the first or
   reference user, and no flow, default or feature is justified by "my team
   does it".
2. **The owner's experience is the evidence base, used as practitioner judgment.**
   They have worked as a software QA engineer in teams of many sizes and
   strictnesses: enterprise teams, large teams, small teams and startups.
   With no interviews planned, that cross-team experience is the best
   first-hand source available. It is treated as an expert's informed
   hypotheses about many teams, not as data from a customer.
3. **Ask for comparisons, not for one workflow.** The useful content is how
   flows, tools and pain differed between the kinds of team they have worked
   in, and what broke at each size. Open question 18 asks that.
4. **Profiles are composites.** The loose, standard and regulated profiles
   (decision 0003) stand for kinds of teams, built from that experience and
   public evidence. They are not descriptions of any real team.
5. **Two axes, not one.** Process strictness (loose to regulated) and
   organisation scale (one person, one team, many teams sharing standards)
   vary independently: a startup can be strict, an enterprise can be loose.
   Scale brings its own needs (many projects, shared steps and fields across
   teams, permissions, roll-up reports, single sign-on). The flows and
   profiles are checked on both axes. Whether scale needs a fourth profile
   is a phase 0 question.

## Consequences

- One person's experience is a single source with its own bias. The
  protections are the ones already in the research plan: desk research and
  complaint threads, the hands-on friction audit of incumbents, an
  independent model reviewing every artifact, and every claim kept a
  hypothesis until a finding backs it. Where the owner's experience and public
  evidence disagree, both are recorded.
- If real teams do become reachable later, the flows are the first thing
  they are shown, and the method constraint (no interviews) is revisited
  then, not now.
- Open question 1 is answered. Whether the owner also uses Mainworthy for their own
  work is a bonus, not a requirement.
